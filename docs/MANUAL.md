# trainbrain manual

Design the model railway layout: lay track on a plan, see it in 3D and as a
schematic, check it, and let Claude drive it over MCP. The track model is a
crate of its own (no UI) so the train management system can run trains on
the same layout file later.

```
cargo run --release -p trainbrain-app -- samples/figure8.layout.json
cargo test
cargo run -p trainbrain-model --example samples   # rewrites samples/
```

With no file the app opens a small sample. `file > new HO / N layout` starts empty.

## Crates

| crate          | what it owns |
|----------------|--------------|
| `trainbrain-model` | geometry (straights, arcs, biarcs), track libraries per scale, the layout document, snapping and joining, heights and grades, the track graph, checks, the generated schematic, JSON files, simulated trains, and `live`: the train link, virtual trains, the tracker |
| `trainbrain-app`   | the imfram app: plan, 3D and schematic views, track library, properties, issues, undo, the MCP server |

## The model

- A piece is a library kind (`ho/s168`, `n/pt6`) or flex track (its own run of straights and arcs), placed by a pose (x, y, heading), optionally mirrored.
- Every piece has ends. Two ends join when they sit on the same spot, facing each other, at the same height. Joins are found, not stored.
- Joined track moves as one (drag, G, R, `move_pieces`) until broken off: B in the plan (or `break_off`) cuts the selection loose from what it joins; once moved apart the break is forgotten, and putting it back rejoins.
- Parallel track (P, or double flex) is stored as a pair with the track beside it: the pair moves together, and J on one of its ends draws both. B unpairs. Paired ends share their height: raise one end and the end beside it follows (as joined ends do), and a ramp along one track ramps the other.
- Each end has a rail-top height; a route ramps between its ends. Grades come from that.
- A buffer stop (`ho/buf`, `n/buf`) ends a line: its far end never joins, is not counted as open, and is a buffer in the schematic.
- Points, crossings and slips have routes and settings (which routes are open).
- Markers sit at a distance along a piece's route: beacons, block boundaries, signals, buffers.
- Files are JSON (`*.layout.json`) with a format version.

Units are millimetres at model scale. The plan is maths-handed: x right, y up, headings counter-clockwise from +x.

The built-in libraries (HO sectional, N sectional) are close to common setrack / unitrack geometry but not copies of any maker's figures: check against the real track before cutting baseboard. A library is plain data, so exact ones can be added.

## Using the editor

Plan (top-down, to scale):
- pick a piece in **Track**, then click on the plan; near an open end (orange) it snaps on. Q / E turn it by 15° (shift: 45°), M mirrors (left point to right), Tab picks which of its ends goes on, Esc or right-click stops
- S draws track through points: click points (shift-click for a straight span), click a free end or press Enter to lay it as flex, smooth through the points (biarcs, with the tangents of a Catmull-Rom spline; a straight span fixes the tangents at its ends to its chord, and gives way to a curve where that would kink). With one open end picked first it carries on from that end
- drag pieces (or G to grab, R to rotate); let go near an open end and they snap onto it
- open ends are connection points: click one to pick it (shift-click a second). J with one picked draws flex that follows the pointer: click a free end to join it there, click anywhere else to lay flex that far and carry on, Esc to stop. J with two ends picked, or two pieces selected, joins them with flex
- P / shift+P lays a parallel track (double track) left / right of the selected run, at the scale's spacing (HO 67 mm, N 33 mm); "left" is left of the way the run was laid. The two stay paired: picking an end lights the end abreast of it, and J draws two flex tracks side by side (onto another pair's ends when you click one of them)
- markers: pick a kind under **Markers** in Track (or K for beacons) and click on the track; they are named B1, B2... Click one to pick it: Properties edits its name, kind and distance along the track; X deletes. Esc stops placing
- The plan opens in lines view (L or the view menu switches to track view and back), like a signal-box panel: one line per track, grey when free, faint in a train's colour where it has a route booked, solid where a train is; points show the set road solid with a dot on the toe and the other road faint; routes ahead dashed. Editing works the same
- Station and beacon names branch off at 45° on short leaders. View (or the plan's top-right box) hides beacons, their names, station names, direction arrows and the direction colours on the track. View > colours… recolours the plan and panels (only changes are kept); view > text sizes… scales interface, train, station and beacon text. Both, and which layers are shown, are remembered in `~/Library/Application Support/layout/prefs.json` (`~/.config/layout/prefs.json` elsewhere). Train bars thin with the zoom down to a single pixel. The 3D view starts closed (view > show 3D); the Track panel's how-to notes are behind its ?.
- grid snapping: shift+G (or view menu, or Properties with nothing selected) snaps what is dragged, placed or drawn to a grid (HO 10 mm, N 5 mm, settable) and turns to 15° steps; joining an end still wins
- lining up: dragging or placing a piece with an open end near the line another open end points along (within 4°) pulls it onto that line, however far off; a blue dashed guide shows it. The same while drawing flex
- E with one open end picked lays a straight on from it: move to set the length (it snaps to open ends ahead, to ends level with its far end, and to the grid), click to lay. Or type a length in Properties
- points and crossovers cut into straight track (flex or sectional, and a run of sectional straights in line counts as one): the point leans to the side the pointer is on; the straight is kept either side as flex
- points and crossovers drop into flex track: with one picked in Track, hover straight flex (away from open ends) and the ghost goes green; click and the flex is cut and the piece put in. A crossover (`xo`, scissors `xx`) needs straight double flex there and faces the other track by itself; Tab turns it end for end (the other hand). `pd` is a point whose leg ends on the next track over, to start double track
- B breaks the selection off from the track it joins and from its pair, so it can be moved alone
- track is tinted by kind: passing loops and refuges green, main lines (worked one way, or bonded double track) blue, single track worked both ways amber, plain track as it is
- double-click a piece to select its whole line: the path a train would take through it with every point in its normal (straight) position, however they are set now. In Properties set which way trains should run along it (→ / ← / either); arrows on the plan show it and new trains face that way. With several pieces selected (a section, shift-click or a box), Properties has the same "trains run" choice for just those, reckoned along the first piece picked
- M mirror, T throws a point (double-click in 3D), X / Delete deletes, shift+D duplicates, ⌘C / ⌘V copy and paste (pasted with their middle at the pointer, on the grid if it is on)
- MMB / alt-drag pans, wheel zooms, Home frames all, F frames the selection
- higher track draws over lower and is tinted from warm grey (ground) to teal (highest)

Properties: position, heading, mirror, point setting, each end's height, each route's length, grade and radius; flex segments are editable; **height locked** keeps a piece out of every height tool's hands. With several pieces selected: set them level, raise or lower them all by a step (grades kept), or ramp them from one end of the run to the other. In the 3D view, drag the selected pieces up or down the screen to change their height; the **soft selection** slider there carries the neighbouring track along with a smooth falloff out to that distance in plan (0 for a hard edit); locked pieces stay put.

3D: Blender hands (MMB orbit, shift-MMB pan, wheel dolly, numpad views). Posts mark raised track. Click picks, double-click throws a point.

Schematic: generated from the plan. Points and crossings are nodes, open ends are buffers, long runs bend at their corners, lines are pulled to 0 / 45 / 90°. Drag a node and it stays where you put it (saved with the layout); `Straighten all` forgets those. The route a point is set to draws bold.

Issues: grades over 3%, flex tighter than the scale's minimum radius, track running into track, crossings with too little clearance (HO 70 mm, N 38 mm), ends that nearly meet but do not join. Click one to select what it is about.

## Simulated trains

The **Trains** tab: `+ train` then click on the track, ▶ run, and drag its speed up. Trains follow the points as set and stop gently at buffer stops (under 60 mm/s). Trains never run into each other: each looks ahead along the track as set and brakes to stop short of any train in its way (half the gap if that one may be coming towards it), then waits ("waits for T2") and goes on when the way clears. A routed train held up for a few seconds looks for a way round whoever is standing in its way (parked trains are kept off all the way; ones just waiting only near the start, as they will have moved). Two trains each waiting for the other: the routed one makes way, off the other's track and where it is going (into a loop, a siding, the other main), lets it pass, then carries on. Trains have a class, express over local over freight (from the name, or set in Trains): a lower one with a route will not throw a point a higher one is coming to in the next few metres, waits for it instead, and one holding up a higher one for a few seconds makes way (that is how an express overtakes a slow local). A point a train has set for itself is a stop signal for the others until it is clear and back to normal, so nobody follows it in. If it loses its route (a point moved, its length changed) it finds another. The simulation runs on the clock, so a hidden window's trains keep going. Driven wrong otherwise they crash and stay put until re-railed: off the end of the track, into a buffer stop too fast, trailing through a point set against them, a point thrown under them They and log every marker they pass (beacons, block boundaries, signals) with the simulated time. Stopped, a train can be turned round.

Route finding: put a train on at a beacon (`+ train at`), pick another under `go to` and press go. It finds the shortest way over the track (`trainbrain_model::route`), reversing where it must: only once all of it, plus a margin, is past the last point, so it can take the other road back. It keeps to the preferred running directions where it can (running against the arrows costs much more), sets each point a stopping distance ahead, never one another train is on or about to reach, and puts points back to normal once it is clear of them, brakes to stop with its head on the beacon, and its route ahead is drawn dashed on the plan. Trains are not saved with the layout. The simulation lives in `trainbrain_model::sim` so the train management system can use it. (This is the exact mode: every train knows just where the others are. See *Realistic mode* below for the one the real layout will run in.)

## Realistic mode: trains known only by what they report

On the real layout the controller will not see the trains. It will send each a throttle and get back only what the loco's electronics report: the beacons its sensor passes, and dead reckoning (mm run) from its wheels. **realistic** in Trains (or `--realistic`, or `set_realism` over MCP) runs the layout that way:

- `trainbrain_model::live::TrainLink` is the trains as the controller reaches them: `throttle(train, mm/s)` and `reverse` out; `Report::Beacon { marker }` and `Report::Moved { mm, speed }` back, plus each train's `Spec` (length, where its sensor sits behind the front). The real ESP32 locos will implement it over radio; until then `Virtual` does, running a second `Sim` in `Mode::Truth` (trains do just what the throttle says, no looking ahead, and really crash).
- The virtual trains are imperfect on purpose (`Noise`): each train's odometer reads a little long or short and its throttle runs a little fast or slow (drawn once per train), reports come every 0.25 s with jitter and latency, a few beacons are missed, fixes are a few mm off. All of it is adjustable in Trains under *noise and trust*, or over MCP, with a seed so runs repeat.
- `Tracker` is the controller's estimate: it moves each train on by its reported speed, corrects by each dead-reckoning report, and snaps to a beacon when one is reported (working out which way the sensor passed it). Its doubt (sigma) grows with distance run on dead reckoning and resets at a beacon (`Trust`). How fast it grows is `Trust::drift`, mm of doubt per mm run (default 0.035, 35 mm a metre): the **doubt growth** slider under the realistic switch in Trains (takes effect at once), `--drift 0.05` on the app, `drift` on `set_realism`, `DRIFT=0.05` on the livebench. Lower lets trains run closer on their estimates but is risky below the real odometer error (`Noise::odo_scale`, ±3% by default); higher widens every margin and means more waiting.
- The planning you already have runs unchanged on the controller's belief: the usual `Sim` in `Mode::Belief`, its trains put each step where the tracker believes them, with a `pad` of doubt either end (k sigma, plus what the train may have run while reports were in flight, never under a floor) that every other train keeps clear of. So trains stop further apart the longer it has been since a beacon, and closer again just past one. Liberal beacons (approaching stations, either side of junctions) are what keeps the margins small.
- Beacon blocks (`trainbrain_model::blocks`): between two beacons the controller only knows a train is *somewhere*, so the track is cut at its beacons and a train never enters a block another train is believed to be in (its envelope, doubt and all, touching it): it holds at the beacon on the way in, its own margin short of it too, until the block is clear. A junction with no beacon of its own is one block with whatever joins it, so beacons either side of points make for small blocks. Occupied blocks are banded on the plan in the train's colour. `beacon blocks` in Trains (or `blocks` on `set_realism`) turns the rule off to compare.
- Two things a believed train does that an exact one need not: it looks along the set points beyond where its route goes only for a train coming at it, never to hold at a beacon block it is not going into (two trains coming to platforms either side of a crossover set between them each looked through it into the other's block and both held); and a point within another train's doubt, which the sim would refuse to throw, counts as held by that train, so the asker waits for it knowingly rather than standing with a throw that never comes; and a point inside its own doubt (just turned round, the points it needs still within where it may be) it gives up on at once and picks its way again, which keeps off such points. Two standing trains each believed, through doubt alone, in a block the other is going into would stare at each other for ever: the lower-numbered goes, the bodies and their margins still keeping them apart.
- The plan draws both on one bar split down the middle: the top half is where each train really is, the bottom half (paler) where the controller believes it, over a full-width paler band out to the ends of its doubt, capped with ticks in the train's colour. Each half comes to a point at its head and is notched to match at its tail, so side by side the two make one arrow and as they drift apart each keeps its half of the point. Each train's name rides on its bar in black capitals, across both halves and curving with it, when zoomed in far enough to fit. Trains shows how far off the belief is now and at worst, how many reports and fixes each train has had, and its margin. A train that really crashes (the belief was too wrong) is marked `really ...`.

Every branch (Harbour, Quarry, Summit, Mill) has a way in from both directions along the main: the junction point it had, and a second point 0.9–1.3 m past the stem facing the other way, its leg curving round to a point 700 mm up the stem (`second_entrance` in `examples/samples.rs`), so a train comes and goes either way without turning round. (The outer main is worked one way, so the other direction means a short run against the arrows, which the planner allows at a cost.) The yards are through yards now: six roads between a ladder at each end off the inner main, one end facing each way (`through_yard`), in at one end and out at the other.

The network sample has a scissors crossover on each side of the ring past the station (as well as the crossovers and scissors it had), and two along the L line between stations, so trains change track either way without running to a terminus to turn: fewer reversals, and the L line rush hour flows.

## Layouts and scenarios

The **layouts** menu opens a sample, smallest first: Figure of eight (two loops, one over the other), Passing loop (a single line with one loop), Showcase (a double-track oval with a yard, a works siding and a climbing branch), Central (eight platforms that are four double-track main lines running straight through, peeling off in eight directions to termini 26–30 m out with two stations each way, 9–10 m apart; scissors within and between every pair at both throats and before every station, so any platform reaches any main either way; three single-track branches with halts, a depot, industrial sidings, and the Circle: a double-track oval out east with four stations, linked to the Eastport and Harbour lines) and Network (the double-track ring with four stations, two yards, a cross line, four branches and the L line). The **scenarios** menu lists the ready-made runs grouped by layout; picking one opens its layout if need be, puts its trains on and starts them.

Scenarios (`trainbrain_model::scenarios`, the Trains panel's dropdown, `--scenario <key>`): on the network, `valley` (4 trains on the single-track Valley line), `l` (3 on the L), `busy` (13 all over, four locals on the L), `park` (busy, then park all at 60 s), `overtake` (two expresses behind a stopper round the ring), `ring` (6 trains, three each way), `summit` (3 on the single-track climb with one loop), `freight` (goods between the four termini, giving way to everything), `rush` (5 on the L), `yards` (8 out of the yards and home again at 90 s), `all` (18 trains); on Central, `central` (seven trains: one on each main and each branch) and `central-rush` (fourteen); on the Showcase, `showcase`. The bench runs any of them by key, or `every`, each on its own layout.

Parking in realistic mode, what the sweeps taught (`tools/sweep.sh`, park all at 20 start times over several noise seeds; `tools/case.sh <park at> <seed>` runs one with its events, `SWEEP=name` on the very binary and layout a sweep took):

- yard ladders want beacons: `blocks::add_waypoints` puts one at the middle of every short plain piece between two points (a ladder's steps), where the approach beacons all land on the next point and are left out. Without them a train runs the whole ladder on dead reckoning and comes onto its road some 350 mm unsure of its place, too unsure to go near the point beyond the road;
- coming home, a train aims to stand centred on its road's beacon but never nearer the points beyond than the hold short of a point set against it lets it come (it stood that hold out for ever, a few mm short of its stopping spot, on Yard A6);
- two parking trains waiting on each other are mostly each held back out of caution alone (standing at a beacon so as not to creep up, short of a block the other's doubt reaches into, by the yard lock, by the plan's order) with nothing in the way of either. The first two times a pair is seen to, one of them **presses on** for 30 s (`Sim::press`: the one nearer home, then the other): past those holds, stopped only by what is really there (a train and its doubt, a point set against it or held by another, a single line claimed). Only if they still wait on each other does one stand down or back off, as before. `NOPRESS=1` on the bench for the old way;
- a train with a point it needs inside its own doubt and no refuge to draw back to (a terminus headshunt: the buffers are nearer than its length and its doubt) sets straight back, or on, along its own track just far enough for the point to be thrown; on its timetable it then picks its platform again by its line's rules;
- while one presses on, the train it was waiting on lets go of the points it has set for itself and not reached, and sets none: holding the junction between them, it is what really is in the way;
- coming home, a train aims its sensor past its road's beacon by its doubt, not by 12 mm: one a few cm short of where it believed itself never read the beacon, came back for it, stopped short by the same few cm again, and shuttled up and down its road with the yard locked behind it. Once it reads the beacon its doubt is next to nothing and it stops there;
- the look-out ahead for other trains covers however far past its stop a train means to go (`Run::past`): a train aiming its sensor well past its road's beacon, after a long spell without a fix, looked no further than the beacon and ran into one standing on its road;
- a train pressing on asks for its points like any other (the plan's order no longer stops it asking), so it is held short of any that cannot be thrown for it, rather than run through one set the other way into the train beyond;
- the pressing on comes round again when the same pair waits on each other afresh, and the one with a way presses on when the other has none; a way back that goes nowhere (to a spot under its own tail) is no way back;
- a believed train on a draw-back is told to stop its doubt short of the end of the run, so the set-back reaches that much further;
- the sweep names a run with a real crash as a failure, whoever got home;
- a train waiting on one ahead of it that has no way yet, whose own plain way home runs on and not back through the waiting one, is in a queue, not a knot: it is left alone (breaking it sent the one in front backing off out onto the main line, nose to nose with the next train coming in);
- a train very unsure of its place (`REFIX_PAD`, a long spell without a fix) with points it needs inside its doubt runs over the nearest beacon for a fix before anything else (`beacon_run`): drawing back clear of one point and then the other as its doubt grew, one shuttled over a stretch with no beacon on it till it was unsure by metres;
- foresight no longer has one train of a line worked in order make way for, or go round, another of that line (it was left off its own track with no platform it may take, and the line stood behind it);
- the bench ends a parking run once nobody at all has moved for 200 s (`STALLEXIT=0` to play on): a deadlocked fleet is planned again and again, and such a run took 40 minutes.
- Foresight will not have one train make way for the same other more than twice in three minutes. Stepping aside before a wait was ripe kept the stand-off breaker from ever having its turn: one train with the yard to itself made way 26 times for another that was only waiting for it to go in.

Tuning for the real train, learnt from the bench (`cargo run --release -p trainbrain-model --example livebench [secs] [seeds] [valley|l|busy...]`, which runs the network scenarios in realistic mode over many noise seeds and reports every real crash with what the controller believed; `EXACT=1` for the old mode to compare, `HARD=1` for twice the noise, `BLOCKS=0`, `NOISE=0`, `SHOWEVENTS=1`, `SHOWTRAIN=L2`; it also reports, per line, how much of the time its trains spend moving, dwelling and just waiting, and the gap to the next train of the line ahead going the same way, sampled once a second: the share of samples under 0.6 m, two train lengths, is "bunched", `GAPDBG=1` lists them; and it lists every stall: a train that has not moved for two minutes while not dwelling, with where it stood, where it was going, whom it was waiting for and its last two events):

- a destination is reached with the sensor just past the beacon (`overrun`), not the head on it, so every arrival is a fix; a terminus never fixed otherwise;
- the controller keeps a train well inside its braking curve (`BELIEF_BRAKE`, 60% of the speed it could brake from) and brings it in under `BUFFER_OK` for the last `SLOW_IN` mm: a real train hears late and cannot stop dead, and a belief still going at speed is not there yet;
- no point is thrown anywhere in a train's margin of doubt, before or behind it, and a train holds its margin short of a point it waits for;
- a train turns round only once its length and `REVERSE_CLEAR` are past the last point, so the point is out of its doubt and can be thrown behind it;
- the network sample has waypoint beacons (`w1`, `w2`...: `trainbrain_model::blocks::add_waypoints`) 250 mm out from every point and every 1.5 m along plain track; they are not platforms (no station), drawn small and unnamed. Without them the long branches ran metres on dead reckoning and the margins grew to the length of a train.

Jams, and how they are worked out (both modes; `game it out` in Trains, `game_out` on `set_realism`):

- **Passing loops / refuges**: tick *passing loop / refuge* on a line in Properties (or `set_refuge`), and a train making way for another goes there sooner than to any plain stretch within 4 m (`Piece::refuge`, `REFUGE_BONUS`). The network sample's station loops and the Valley and Summit Halt loops are marked.
- **Gaming it out**: when two trains have waited on each other for `WAIT_REPLAN` seconds, the controller plays each way out on a copy of everything, 30 s ahead at a 0.1 s step (this one makes way to its refuge, the other does, this one goes another way round, both wait), scores each by mm run and arrivals (a crash is ruinous, the class rule a nudge), and does the best (`Sim::game_pair`; the event log says what it chose and why). Off, it does the first thing that works, as before.
- **Foresight** (`foresight` in Trains, on `set_realism`): every 5 s the controller plays the next 60 s out on a copy of everything, on another core, as things stand. If it sees a jam coming (a train stuck waiting on another for 8 s, more than a few seconds from now), it plays the ways of heading it off now (hold one at its platform 12 s longer so they meet at the station, make way early into a refuge, go another way round) and does the one that plays clearly better than letting it happen (`Sim::foresee`, `FORESIGHT_MARGIN`; the event log says what and why). The verdict is applied only if the trains concerned are still going where they were when the copy was taken. Trains book only the next `BOOK_AHEAD` (5 m) of their planned route; beyond that the others plan through, and where they meet, the safety layer, the single-line claims, the beacon blocks and the gaming settle it. (The coordinator's park and resume moves are still booked whole.)
- Neither the gaming nor foresight holds a frame: in the app both play out on worker threads on a copy of everything (`live::Games`, `Sim::game_sync` off) and the verdict is applied when it comes back, if the trains concerned are still about what they were. Headless runs and tests play them out inline. The simulation clock also never runs more than two seconds a frame however high the speed is set.
- **Patience**: a train waiting for a platform because every way there is booked by other trains' routes waits `PLATFORM_WAIT` seconds, then goes anyway and lets the safety layer and the gaming sort the meeting out. Before, eight trains leaving the yards together would all wait on each other's bookings.

`cargo test -p trainbrain-model live::` runs the headless checks: no noise means no error; a drifting odometer is pulled back by beacons; a faster train on a line behind a slower one stops short on estimates alone; one holds at a beacon while the block ahead is taken, and the two are never in one block by the truth; two L trains run their timetables with no real crash.

## Lines

A line is a named, ordered run of stations, worked end to end or round, saved with the layout (`trainbrain_model::lines`). A train belongs to a line and has a class, and its timetable follows from the two: a **local** calls at every station on the line, an **express** only where the line says expresses stop (and at the ends), a **freight** only at the ends and yards. Change the line, or the train's class, and the timetable changes with it; a train with no line keeps a timetable of its own.

A stop can be flagged **goods** (docks, works, sidings, a mine): freights call there and passenger trains never do; a freight runs between the line's goods stops and yards (and the ends, if that leaves it fewer than two). The network has Docks off the Harbour branch, Quarry Sidings, Summit Mine and Mill Works, and a grey Goods line over them all from yard to yard; Central has Harbour Docks, Southport Works and Westmoor Quarry on a Goods line from the depot.

In Trains, **Lines** lists the layout's lines (colour, pattern, stations with their express tick) and makes new ones: name it, add stations in order, tick the express stops, pick end to end or round. Each train's card has a line picker and its class: pick a line and it sets off on it as that class; its plate on the plan reads "L1 · L express", and it is drawn in its line's colour (the route ahead, the block it holds and the single line it claims too). Over MCP: `lines`, `set_line` (make or change a line), `delete_line`, and `set_train` with `line` and `class`. Every sample has its lines (the L, the Valley, Summit, the ring's Inner and Outer, the branches; Central's two mains and three locals; the Showcase's Main and Branch), and the scenarios put trains on them by class.

## Stations and timetables

A station is a named group of beacons, its platforms, saved with the layout. `stations > from beacon names` makes them ("South 1" and "South A" are platforms of South, "Yard A1" of Yard A); or pick a beacon and give it a station in Properties. Station names show on the plan over their platforms.

A train's timetable is the stations it calls at, in turn: in its card (Trains, or Properties when it is picked) add stops, set how long it stands at each, tick "round again" to loop, and start. At each station it takes a free platform (not one another train is standing at or going to), the shortest way there, stands its time, and goes on. Its name on the plan says where it is going or standing.

A timetable runs its stops once, round again, or there and back: down the stops, then back up them, for ever, like a line worked end to end (the network's L line: 8th Av to Canarsie and back, turning on the scissors past each terminus onto the other track).

Single track worked both ways: double-click a line and tick "single track" in Properties (or `set_single`); it applies to the stretch between points. One train at a time: a train claims the stretch before it leaves the station before it (or, if it has no timetable, as it comes up to it), and the others wait at their station, in the passing loop, until it is off; then they go the other way. Single track is drawn with amber ticks, in the colour of the train that holds it. The network's Summit branch is single track with a passing loop at Summit Halt; the Valley line (to Mill) is single track with passing loops at Ashby, Brook, Cross and Dale. The L line has nine stations, 8th Av to Canarsie, and is linked to the ring.

Yards: a train's yard is its own, but any road in it will do: on park all each takes the free road of its yard quickest to reach from where it is (`Sim::park_any_road`); `park_all nearest=true` lets it take the nearest free road of any yard (measured slower on the network, so not the default). Every train has a home yard road (a platform of a station named "Yard ..."), kept in the layout by the train's name and picked in its card (or `set_home`); one without gets the first free road. Yard A has a seventh road for the fourth L train: a stub siding off the yard's access line (the one under road A1 between the ladders' feet), on the main-line side at x = 12 m, its point's toe facing the left foot where the L line comes in, the beacon "Yard A7" and a buffer at its end (`examples/addroad.rs` added it to the sample; the two ladders leave no room for another through road, A6 is too short to cut, and the roads are 67 mm apart). On park all a stub siding is its own train's for the whole plan, so nobody else turns round in it; a train the plan finds no way for keeps off others' stub sidings and bodies when it goes its plain way, and after four plans without a way takes its plain way round only the parked trains and queues behind the rest on the safety layer. **park all** in Trains (or `park_all`) sends every train home, putting its timetable aside; **resume** gives them back.

On resume, each train starts from the platform at its first stop it can go on from the right way to the stop after (not the other track's, which would leave it facing a long way round, in everyone's way), and trains sharing a line are spread along it: each stands a headway longer than the one before at its first stop (the line's round trip at cruise shared among them, 15–150 s; held there rather than in the yard, whose throat lets them out one at a time anyway), so they run spaced out instead of nose to tail; and a ring of trains each waiting on the next, however long, is broken by the latest in the order handing its route back. `cargo run --release -p trainbrain-model --example routecheck -- <piece> <mm> fwd|back <marker>` prints the way the planner would give a train standing there (or why none), on the network sample. Directional track comes in three tiers (`Piece::tier`, the "held" row under the direction buttons, `set_direction`'s `tier`): **suggested** (the wrong way costs three times the length, so a train takes it when it is clearly shorter, and it is no reason to wait), **strongly suggested** (the default, and what every directed piece already was) and **enforced** (no route ever runs against it, not even to make way). Routes weigh the wrong way along strongly directional track at forty times its length (and a held-up train keeps its right-way route and waits, rather than take a way round against the arrows, until it has stood 45 s; the jam game-out offers no such way round either), each reversal at 4 m and each set of points run over at 0.5 m, so a train takes the right-way, straight-through way where there is one; the fleet planner counts a reversal and each junction against a way the same spirit (15 s and 3 s). How evenly a line's trains are spread round it is measured (`Sim::line_positions`, each train's place round the line's cycle, out and back for a shuttle line; `Sim::line_spread`, the smallest gap between neighbours as a share of the even gap, 1 being perfectly even and 0 two trains together): the lines panel shows it, the `lines` MCP tool reports it with each train's position, and the bench prints each line's mean and worst (`SPREADDBG=1` for the positions every 30 s; the bench's `saved` pseudo-scenario runs the layout's own trains from where they were saved, as the app starts them). A train put on a line takes it up facing the way it stands: at one of its stations, it has arrived there and goes on toward the neighbour it can reach without turning round (back the way it came at an end); between stations, the nearest stop it can reach without turning round is next. Lines are **worked in order** by default (`Line::ordered`, the "in order" tick in the lines panel, `set_line`'s `ordered`): a train of such a line goes to its own platform at each station, the one straight ahead (no running against the arrows, no reversing on the way), and only that one, queueing behind a train of its line going its way that has it (the safety layer holds it off; it goes in when that leaves) rather than taking the other track's platform to get past; it waits for a platform held by anyone else; standing at a station, it lets a train of its line that came in before it, going its way, leave first (for a minute at most: that one may be stuck on something else); it follows such a train onto a single line instead of waiting for the claim; and held up behind one it just follows, with no gaming it out and no making way; it works in blocks: it does not leave a station for the next while a train of its line going its way is still at that next station, at any station it would skip on the way (an express behind a local), or on the way to one (one train between stations, and the dwell spreads them out; where the next station lies on a dead end, the whole dead end is one block for the line either way); and it never turns back early. When every train round a line stands at the next one's station, each waiting for the next to clear it (a ring with as many trains as stations), they all go on together, each into the section the one ahead is leaving, since none ever would otherwise. Only its own line's trains hold it to its own platform: one held by another line's train leaves it the usual choice of the rest. Its way to its own platform never turns round where the plain way there does not (the planner, keeping off others' routes, would otherwise send a ring express back the other way round the ring to get past; it goes its plain way and queues instead). Where no platform at a station is reachable the right way at all (a branch joining a ring worked one way), the usual choice stands, against the arrows and all. Its own platform at a terminus is reached the line's proper way round (out past the platform and back through the crossover, never setting back along a one-way platform), so the other platform is left for the train behind. Off, every train acts for itself as before, which is what lets an express pass a stopper on the ring. Every train on a timetable keeps one (`Service::due`, `due_next`, `late`): as it sets off from a stop it is given a due time out of the next (when it was due out of this one, the way's length at cruise with the start and end pieces only as far as they are used, the time to get up to speed, a tenth over and a second for slack, and the dwell); coming in, it is due out at that time or after its dwell, whichever is later, and its lateness is the difference (early is negative). Early, it holds to its due time; late, it goes as soon as its dwell is done and the timetable moves on from there. The card shows "due out in n s", "on time", "n s late" or "n s early"; the `trains` MCP tool reports `late_s` and `due_out_in_s`; the bench prints each line's mean and worst lateness. Lines dwell 12 s at a stop by default, which spaces trains along a line; and trains keep their distance on the way: a train whose dwell is up holds at the station until a headway (the line's round trip shared among its trains: the running at cruise, getting up to speed out of every stop and the dwell at every stop, 15–300 s, `Sim::line_headway`) after the last train of its line left that station the same way (`Sim::departed`), so a pair that has bunched up spreads out again at the next stop rather than running nose to tail. A there-and-back train that has waited a minute for a platform at the next station (two stations full of trains each bound for the other's platforms, on a single line) turns back early and comes to it later. A train does not set off from a station onto track another train is coming the other way along (the single line between stations, or a terminus throat): the one coming in is in first, which is the capacity rule of a line of passing loops, that trains cross at stations. (On the hand-edited network the Valley's loop platforms at Brook and Cross are worked both ways, so two trains can swap there, and the straights between its stations are three times what they were, with waypoints along them; the generator's `samples` example adds waypoints to the hand-edited layout wherever its track has grown.) A believed train plans its reversals with the points it then needs beyond all its doubt, and on the move takes no way over points just in front of it that it may really be on; no point is thrown under the nose of a rolling train, plan or no plan; and the plan's gates count passes, so a train that reverses beyond a piece and comes back over it waits its turn the second time too; the route search itself keeps a believed train off points within its doubt set the other way (so it finds the way that needs no throw, rather than one it could never take); and a train on its timetable that ends up facing a train the coordinator is moving steps aside for it rather than picking the same way again. A train whose make-way run was taken from it before it got clear (left marked as making way, with nothing to carry on from) carries on to where it was going after the usual pause. With enforced directions a train cannot turn back mid-line at all (not even the short wrong-way move through a crossover that a strong direction allows at a price): it turns at the termini, which are dead ends and exempt; so a train out of sequence on an enforced line, needing the station behind it, has a long way round; and a train picking a platform never takes one on enforced one-way track it could not go on from to the stop after (the other track's platform when its own is full), which is how it got out of sequence. Two trains of one line worked in order never game out or make way for each other (L4 once made way for L3 onto the eastbound track at 3rd Av and set off into its face): they queue. A train on its timetable that hands its route back because it stands in the way of a train waiting its turn with the coordinator stands down 30 s before picking again, so that train can be given a route (V3 and V4 stood nose to nose on the Valley after a resume, V3 re-picking the same way every 12 s); and a ring of waits that runs through such a train's task is broken like any other. A train standing at a beacon (a platform, a yard road) whose next hold ahead is closer than its own length and a clearance stays where it is, rather than creep up to the hold and stand with its tail across the stopping spot the next train is given (L3 did that out of Canarsie 1, waiting for L4, and L1 was sent into it); a believed train following another stops its own doubt sooner; and a train never runs through points another train holds set against its way, plan or no plan (under the plan it would follow them in behind that train). The `trains` MCP tool now reports each train's `waiting_for`, `stalled_s`, `made_way` and coordinator `task`, for finding out why one stands. Park all and resume are run by a coordinator, like a signaller setting routes: each train finishes the run it is on, then goes in turn: when its whole route is free it is reserved for it (others may follow it the same way, never meet or cross it); when not, it sets off anyway as far as is free, booking only that, and stops where it is off points and off the routes of trains ahead of it in the order, going on as more comes free; a train in the way of the next move steps aside once, off that route; two that end up waiting on each other, the later hands its route back. Every train with a route set reserves it, so trains on their timetables pick routes that do not meet others too. The order the fleet goes home in is planned, not guessed: on park all, nine orderings (nearest first, farthest first, the yards in turn, and shuffles) are each played out 420 s ahead at super speed on another thread, a core each (`Sim::plan_park`), and the one that has everyone home soonest with the least reversing is taken (`take_park_order`); the coordinator holds its grants the few seconds that takes. On the network's busy scenario this took the average park from 184 s to 162 s and the worst from 286 s to 209 s, and the play-out's forecast is within about 15 s of what then happens. Foresight dispatches as well as dodges: when a jam is coming it also tries holding any train standing at a platform a little longer (not just the two concerned), and weighs a jam still to come by how soon it comes. All the play-outs share a thread pool of half the machine's cores (four at most), and foresight waits four times as long as its last play-out took before the next, so the UI and the running sim keep their cores. Each card says what the coordinator is doing with its train. Park all now runs by **Ordered Convergence** (`Sim::ordered`, on; `ORDERED=0` on the bench for the coordinator above), which is what the design in the project's "Parking without gridlock" notes came to: once every train stands, the fleet's ways home are planned together in space and time (`timeplan.rs`: a piece is held from the moment a head comes onto it until the tail is clear, a standing train also holds what lies within 320 mm of its ends, single lines are held whole for a crossing; trains are planned in order of who is in whose way, nearest home first, a few milliseconds the lot); then each train drives its way on the safety layer, and may run onto a piece only once every train due there before it has been and gone, whatever the clock says. The planning runs on another thread in the app (the sim asks for a plan and takes it up when it comes, if the same trains are still to park), and a search gives up after 60,000 states, so a boxed-in train cannot hold a frame. The fleet is planned again every 8 s from where the trains really are, two trains waiting on each other for 10 s have the one with further to go stand down (and, if it keeps happening, back off to a loop), and a train the plan can find no way for stands until the next plan (and, left without one for two plans running while the track itself is clear, goes its plain way on the safety layer, as the coordinator would send it: the table can be full of parked trains' reach and others' claims round a yard throat though nothing is actually in the way). On the network's busy scenario, 19 start times: every one parks, 153 s on average (the coordinator: 18 of 19, 161 s), with the same reversals. An experimental **staged** parking (`Sim::staged`, off; `STAGED=1` on the bench) sends everyone to their yard at once on the safety layer alone, queueing at the yard's pointwork (`yard_zone`) and going in one at a time with only the way in reserved; it never sets off onto a single line another train is on, and a train meeting another nose to nose backs off to a loop. It parks the busy network cleanly but slower than the coordinator so far (about 225 s against 150 s), the time going into trains backing off from each other on the approaches. (`samples/network.layout.json` is now the hand-edited working layout; the generator leaves it alone and writes its own version to `samples/network-generated.layout.json`, which the geometry-dependent tests use.) `cargo run --release -p trainbrain-model --example parkbench [from to every]` runs park all and resume on the network sample, headless, from many start times, and reports how long they took, reversals, dodges and crashes (`PARK_PLAN=0` for the plain nearest-first order, `PARK_FIXED=1` to keep every train to its usual road).

Click a train on the plan to pick it: it is ringed, and Properties shows its card. The view menu also turns layers of the plan on and off: train plates, routes ahead, the true trains, the estimated trains with their margins, the held beacon blocks, beacon names and station names (the picked train always shows). **follow** (in Trains, the view menu, or ⇧F on the plan; `follow_train` over MCP) keeps the picked train in the middle of the plan as it runs; pan or untick to let go. Q over any panel shows it alone (full window); Q again puts the panels back (not over the plan while placing track, where Q turns the piece).

## MCP

The app serves MCP over HTTP on `http://127.0.0.1:7878/mcp` while it runs (`--port N` to move it, `--no-mcp` to turn it off). To point Claude Code at it:

```
claude mcp add --transport http trainbrain http://127.0.0.1:7878/mcp
```

**Recording**: the record menu (record 30 s, 2 min, until stopped; stop; show the last recording in Finder) and the `record`, `record_stop` and `record_status` MCP tools film the app's main window to an mp4: the window is rendered offscreen at the recording's rate (10 frames a second by default), the pixels read back and piped to `ffmpeg` (`brew install ffmpeg`), so no screen-recording permission is involved. Files go to `recordings/` beside the samples unless `file` says otherwise. `python3 tools/jamwatch.py` watches the running app for jams: it polls `trains` every 30 s and appends every stall (a train that has not moved for two minutes with somewhere to go: where, whither, waiting for whom, its task and last events) to `tools/jamwatch.log`, with a status line in `tools/jamwatch.status.json`.

Tools: `get_layout`, `list_track`, `new_layout`, `add_piece`, `attach_piece`, `add_run`, `drop_piece`, `join_ends`, `parallel`, `move_pieces`, `break_off`, `delete_pieces`, `mirror_pieces`, `set_point`, `set_height`, `grade`, `add_marker`, `delete_marker`, `line`, `set_direction`, `stations`, `set_station`, `set_service`, `set_single`, `set_realism`, `follow_train`, `park_all`, `park`, `resume_all`, `set_home`, `add_train`, `set_train`, `navigate`, `remove_train`, `run_sim`, `trains`, `check`, `schematic`, `select`, `undo`, `redo`, `save`, `open`. They act on the open layout on the UI thread, so edits show at once and undo like any other.
